How calls reach us

Over a SIP trunk on the number you already own. Your calls stay on your own number, in and out, and there are no per-minute forwarding charges from your operator. It is the only route we connect, so there is nothing to choose here. The steps are below.

How to get your trunk, step by step

This is the part that is not on our public site. Follow it once and your line is done.

  1. Call your operator's business line and ask for a SIP trunk on your existing number. Vodafone Business, Orange Business, Telecom Egypt (WE) and e& all sell this as a standard product. Ask for it by name. You keep the number: this is not a new line. Say it like this: "I have a number with you and I want a SIP trunk on it, keeping the same number."
  2. Tell them two things when they ask. You want IP authentication or registration credentials, and you want the trunk to allow both inbound and outbound on your existing number. Ask them to confirm the caller ID on outbound calls will show your own number. If they ask how many concurrent calls: start with two. You can raise it later, and it is what you pay for.
  3. They send you a server address, a username and a password. Usually by email within a few working days. Paste the first two into the form beside this guide. Keep the password with your own records; we do not store it.
  4. Ask them to allow our media addresses. Some operators lock a trunk to specific addresses. If yours does, send them the addresses shown in your account under Numbers. Without this the trunk connects but no audio passes, which is the single most common reason a first test call is silent.
  5. Make one test call before you switch it on. Ring your own number from a mobile and listen. If it rings but nobody answers, the trunk is not registered. If it answers but is silent, it is the step above.
Stuck at any step? Send us the operator's reply exactly as they wrote it and we will tell you what to say back. Most trunks are live within a week of the first call.
